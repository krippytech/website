Everyday IT · Microsoft 365 & Email

Preserve the mailbox without preserving the employee's sign-in.

Departures become messy when mailbox access, account access, retention, delegation, and licensing are treated as the same thing.Decide what the business needs to keep, then choose the mailbox and permission model that supports it.

Start with the business requirement

Before changing anything, determine whether someone needs historical mail, future incoming mail, send-as capability, or only a temporary review period.

History

Keep prior correspondence

Confirm whether the organization needs the existing mailbox contents retained and who is authorized to review them.

Future mail

Decide what new messages should do

New mail may need to continue arriving, be delegated to a successor, or be handled through another approved business process.

Identity

Do not hand out the former user's password

Mailbox access should normally be provided through approved delegation rather than preserving or sharing the departed employee's interactive sign-in.

A safer operational pattern

The exact licensing and retention rules can vary by tenant and Microsoft policy, so verify the current environment before making irreversible changes.

1

Confirm the mailbox still exists and is healthy

Check the object, mailbox state, and expected content before changing account or licensing settings.

2

Choose the intended mailbox model

If the mailbox is becoming a team-held historical resource, a shared-mailbox design may be appropriate. Do not assume conversion is always the answer without checking size, archive, retention, hold, and licensing requirements.

3

Grant named users only what they need

Use mailbox permissions instead of generic credentials. Reading and sending are separate capabilities.

4

Verify from the successor's Outlook or web client

Confirm the delegated mailbox appears, historical mail is present, and sending behavior matches the request before removing any remaining recovery path.

When to stop and escalate

Retention

Legal hold, archive, or compliance requirements exist

Do not change mailbox type or licensing until retention obligations are understood.

Recovery

Older mail is already missing

If content appears absent, stop treating the task as simple delegation. Recovery, backup, retention, or restore procedures may be required.

Unclear

The owner cannot say what should be preserved

Get a business decision before changing the mailbox. Technical convenience should not decide retention policy.

Keep the business data. Remove the unnecessary identity risk.
